# Suggested reviewers

Each name below is an author of work the manuscript cites, so the basis for the
suggestion is checkable rather than asserted. Names and, where the Crossref
record carries them, affiliations were read from the record of the cited paper
rather than recalled. Contact addresses are in the corresponding papers; the
author fills them into the submission system, and confirms before submitting
that there is no prior collaboration or other conflict with any name kept.

Two of the suggestions are deliberate: the group behind the tool this work is
measured against. The comparison in the manuscript was transcribed from their
published source and reports that neither rule dominates, so it was written to
be checked by the people best placed to check it.

## Deduplication and automated evidence synthesis

**Kaitlyn Hair** and **Emily S. Sena** (ASySD; Hair K, Bahor Z, Macleod M, Liao
J, Sena ES, *BMC Biology* 21:189, 2023, doi:10.1186/s12915-023-01686-z). They
built the benchmark the accuracy claims are scored on and the decision rule the
manuscript compares against clause by clause. No one is better placed to judge
whether that comparison is fair, and the manuscript credits the one mechanism it
borrows from their tool in the ablation table and in the source.

**Wichor M. Bramer** (Erasmus MC; Bramer WM, Rethlefsen ML, Kleijnen J, Franco
OH, *Systematic Reviews* 6:245, 2017, doi:10.1186/s13643-017-0644-y, and the
deduplication method in *JMLA* 104(3), doi:10.3163/1536-5050.104.3.014). His
work supplies both the multi-database recall argument in the motivation and a
published deduplication procedure, so he can assess the retrieval claims and the
merging claims together.

## Search systems and bibliographic data quality

**Michael Gusenbauer**, Institute of Innovation Management, Johannes Kepler
University Linz (Gusenbauer M, Haddaway NR, *Research Synthesis Methods* 11(2),
doi:10.1002/jrsm.1378). The source registry's principal and supplementary tiers
derive from his assessment of which search systems are suitable for evidence
synthesis, including the one entry the manuscript marks as a discipline
convention that his list does not support.

**Martijn Visser**, Centre for Science and Technology Studies, Leiden
University (Visser M, van Eck NJ, Waltman L, *Quantitative Science Studies* 2(1),
doi:10.1162/qss_a_00112). His large-scale comparison of bibliographic data
sources is the right background for judging the cross-database overlap and
metadata-completeness results.

## Reporting standards

**Melissa L. Rethlefsen** (PRISMA-S; Rethlefsen ML et al., *Systematic Reviews*
9:39, 2020, doi:10.1186/s13643-020-01542-z). The PRISMA-S appendix generator is
the part of the software with the most direct methodological consequence, and
she is a lead author of the standard it implements.

## Research software for reviews

**Martin J. Westgate**, Fenner School of Environment and Society, Australian
National University (revtools; Westgate MJ, *Research Synthesis Methods* 10(4),
doi:10.1002/jrsm.1374). He has published review software in the same space and
can judge the design and the reuse claims from the position of someone who has
maintained a comparable package.

## Note on scope

The list is deliberately weighted towards evidence synthesis methodology rather
than general software engineering, because the claims most in need of expert
scrutiny are methodological: what counts as a duplicate, which databases a
review must search, and what a search appendix has to record.
